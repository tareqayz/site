Most training plans pick one thing and get very good at it. This log does the opposite: it trains the whole body, every capacity, and writes it all down in public, one day at a time.

## the eight domains

Every day is tagged with the capacities it trained. They're the same eight axes as **Unorthodox**, the app I'm building to test and track them.

| code  | domain             | the short version                       |
| ----- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| `STR` | strength           | maximum force you can produce           |
| `PWR` | power              | force × speed, explosiveness            |
| `END` | muscular endurance | repeating effort without breaking down  |
| `CAR` | cardio endurance   | your aerobic engine                     |
| `FLX` | flexibility        | passive range: how far you can go       |
| `MOB` | mobility           | active range: controlling the end of it |
| `BAL` | balance            | stability and body awareness            |
| `AGI` | agility & reaction | neural speed and coordination           |

## how a day is written

- **the session**: what I actually did, sets and reps and all
- **why**: what I was going for, and what I noticed
- **sources**: where the idea came from. Each source has its own note, so you can see every day it shaped.

## the garden

At the bottom of the homepage there's a garden with a patch for every day of the year. When I post a day, a plant grows in its patch. The first domain I tag picks what grows: an oak for strength, a sunflower for power, a pine for endurance, reeds for cardio, a willow for flexibility, a fern for mobility, a stack of stones for balance, a dandelion for agility. Each plant is drawn from its own date, so no two are the same.

When I miss a day, its patch stays bare soil, and I don't go back and fake it. There's no streak counter. The point is to keep showing up, not to never miss.

The sand at the top is just for breathing. Move your cursor through it.
